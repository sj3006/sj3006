# Every measured number this work takes from outside it

Nothing in this project is fitted to data generated by this project. Everything
below came from somebody else's published measurement, and this file says which
measurement, where in the article it appears, and which module holds it.

If you are checking this work, these are the numbers to check first: the
reproduction harness in `repro/` verifies that the paper and the code quote the
same figures, but it cannot verify that either matches the article.

## 1. Melt pool geometry, for the calibration

**Weaver, J. S. et al. (2024), "Cross-Sectional Melt Pool Geometry of Laser
Scanned Tracks and Pads on Nickel Alloy 718 for the 2022 Additive Manufacturing
Benchmark Challenges", *Integrating Materials and Manufacturing Innovation* 13,
363-379.** DOI 10.1007/s40192-024-00355-5

From **Table 4**, seven bare-plate single-track conditions on IN718: laser
power, scan speed, D4-sigma spot diameter, and the mean melt pool width and
depth over six cross-sections each. Also the expanded uncertainties, U at k = 2.

Held in `src/calibrate.py`, `WEAVER_POINTS`, with the uncertainties in the
comment above it. These seven conditions are the only data the forward model is
fitted to.

## 2. Pad melt pool depth, for the gas modulation profile

**The same article, Table 5.** Pads scanned on bare IN718 under a 4.3 m/s argon
cross-flow. The Y-pad is scanned along the gas-flow axis, so its odd and even
tracks run anti-parallel and parallel to the flow; the X-pad is scanned across
the flow and serves as a control.

Held in `src/weaver_pads.py`.

| Y-pad cross-section | anti-parallel, um | parallel, um | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.6 mm | 175.2 | 120.0 | -31.5 % |
| 1.5 mm | 175.1 | 123.5 | -29.5 % |
| 1.9 mm | 174.0 | 124.7 | -28.3 % |
| 2.9 mm | 170.1 | 137.1 | -19.4 % |
| | | **mean** | **-27.2 %** |

X-pad control: -2.41 % and -4.71 %. Standard deviations across all twelve rows
of Table 5: 3.9 to 10.7 um.

Weaver et al. attribute the reduction to plume by-products attenuating,
scattering and lensing the beam, and report that none of the four challenge
submissions predicted it. This model represents the attenuation alone.

## 3. Powder absorptivity, for the layer fault

**Trapp, J. et al. (2017), "In situ absorptivity measurements of metallic
powders during laser powder-bed fusion additive manufacturing", *Applied
Materials Today* 9.** DOI 10.1016/j.apmt.2017.08.006

Powder absorptivity measured calorimetrically in situ at 1070 nm, about **twice**
that of a bare polished disc for roughly 100 um layers.

Held in `src/layer_couplings.py` as `POWDER_SOLID_RATIO = 2.0`, with the
derivation of the coupling constant from it.

*Caveat recorded in that module:* the saturation length is taken as twice the
powder d50, which radiative-transfer treatments of metal powder beds support in
order of magnitude but which is not itself a measurement from this article. The
coupling is swept across the range that assumption spans, in
`src/coupling_sweep.py`.

## 4. The keyhole correction and its parameterisation

**Honarmandi, P. et al. (2021), "A rigorous test and improvement of the
Eagar-Tsai model for melt pool characteristics in laser powder bed fusion
additive manufacturing", *Additive Manufacturing* 47, 102300.**
DOI 10.1016/j.addma.2021.102300

Three things taken from this work, all in `src/eagar_tsai.py`:

- the **Gladush-Smurov keyhole depth** term, their Eq. 5
- the **keyholing criterion** depth >= width / 1.5, applied to measured
  dimensions
- the practice of treating conductivity and heat capacity as **calibration
  parameters** rather than constants, because a constant-property conduction
  model absorbs the physics it omits into the properties it has

Also their reported **pair correlations of 0.93 to 0.96** between fitted
absorptivity, conductivity and heat capacity, which the paper's limitations
section uses: it is the same identifiability pathology the paper studies,
appearing in its own calibration.

Their fitted values, for comparison with this work's: absorptivity 0.60-0.64,
conductivity 39-62 W/mK, heat capacity 849-1490 J/kgK, keyhole factor 1.32-3.10,
across four alloys. Every value fitted here falls below their range, which is
recorded as a limitation rather than explained away.

## 5. Photodiode response to process parameters, for the external check

**Reijonen, J. (2024), "Effect of laser parameters and shielding gas flow on
co-axial photodiode-based melt pool monitoring signals in laser powder bed
fusion", *Additive Manufacturing Letters* 11, 100244.**

From their Table 2 and the surrounding text: the scan-speed response as
`y = a x^-b` with **b = 0.337 at 400 W** over 400-1200 mm/s, the statement that
the exponent **grows with laser power** and is "almost non-existent" at 100 W,
and the power response as a **positive linear** fit whose intercept is not
quoted. Material 316L, 30 um layers, detection through a shortpass below 800 nm.

Held in `src/reijonen_check.py`. The model partly fails this check and the paper
says so; the module explains why in terms of the physics the model omits.

## 6. Melt pool width under reduced gas flow

**Reijonen, J. et al. (2020), "On the effect of shielding gas flow on porosity
and melt pool geometry in laser powder bed fusion additive manufacturing",
*Additive Manufacturing* 32, 101030.**

Reduced gas flow gives loss of penetration together with **increased** melt pool
width, attributed to attenuation and scattering in the plume. This model gives a
width *decrease*, and `src/scattering_channel.py` measures what it would take to
reverse that and what it would cost the paper's exactness claim.

## 7. Layer thickness variation in a real build

**Liu, T. et al. (2022), "In-situ infrared thermographic inspection for local
powder layer thickness measurement in laser powder bed fusion", *Additive
Manufacturing* 55, 102873.**

Local powder layer thickness measured through a build, with the variation
organised by **part support** - about 100 um at an unsupported overhang edge
against 200 um at the specimen centre - rather than by the recoater cycle this
work assumes. Used in `src/profile_robustness.py` to test what happens when a
modulation profile follows the wrong build variable.

## 8. Constant-energy-density melt pool variation

**Guo, Q. et al. (2019), "In-situ characterization and quantification of melt
pool variation under constant input energy density in laser powder bed fusion
additive manufacturing process", *Additive Manufacturing* 28, 600-609.**

At constant energy density the melt pool varies by orders of magnitude and
changes melting regime, which they attribute to absorption varying with vapour
depression geometry. Used qualitatively in `src/validation.py` as a check the
model partly passes: the existence, direction and regime transition reproduce,
the magnitude does not, because the mechanism they identify is absent from a
conduction solution.

## What is not measured

Everything else. In particular:

- **The modulation profiles** of paper Section 3.5 are physical claims about how
  each fault's severity varies across a build. One of the five is calibrated
  against item 2 above, and found conservative. The other four are asserted and
  swept; `src/profile_robustness.py` is the whole of the defence.
- **The layer-to-conductivity coupling** is an estimate with no measurement
  behind it, declared as such in `src/faults.py` and swept in
  `src/coupling_sweep.py`.
- **The sensor noise figures** are derived from each instrument's transduction
  chain under one assumption - that each radiometric channel carries the same
  fractional noise - rather than measured. Only ratios enter the analysis, so
  that assumption cancels; see `src/noise.py` and
  `explainers/11-deriving-sensor-noise.md`.
- **The scan plan** is synthetic. It is built from quantities a machine knows
  before a build, but it is not a real sliced file.
