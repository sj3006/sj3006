\begin{abstract}
In-situ monitoring is standard on metal additive manufacturing systems, and
classifiers report high accuracies for detecting defects from photodiode and
imaging signals. Those results answer a question about outcomes: which defect
formed. Specifying hardware requires an answer one step upstream --- which process
\emph{causes} a configuration can separate in principle, and which remain
confounded whatever classifier is applied. Where two causes produce signatures
differing by less than the sensor noise, no volume of training data distinguishes
them: the limit is structural, not algorithmic. This paper applies structural and
quantitative diagnosability analysis, mature in automotive and aerospace process
control, to laser powder bed fusion. A calibrated reduced-order thermal model
gives the response of a coaxial suite to five injected faults --- laser power and
scan speed deviation, thin powder layers, degraded shielding gas flow and
geometry-driven heat accumulation --- with separability measured as an angle
between noise-whitened signatures and each sensor's relative noise derived from
its transduction chain rather than assumed. Laser power and gas degradation prove
\emph{exactly} confounded instantaneously, by algebra rather than noise, and
seven candidate observables leave the attained rank at three against a structural
bound of four. Regressing the same three sensors onto known build context raises
the rank to five and the worst pair from $0.00^\circ$ to $24.90^\circ$ at no
hardware cost, outperforming two idealised direct measurements of the causes
themselves. Matrices are reported for five configuration classes found on current
commercial machines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) builds a part by melting thousands of short track
segments per layer, and a modest excursion in any of several independent inputs
can produce a rejected part. Manufacturers have responded by fitting in-situ
monitoring as standard --- coaxial photodiodes, melt pool cameras, pyrometers,
increasingly acoustic and powder-bed imaging --- and a large literature maps
those signals onto defects with high reported accuracy.

Almost all of that work answers a question about \emph{outcomes}: given the
signals, which defect formed? That matters for part acceptance, but not for the
machine builder specifying hardware or the process engineer facing a drifting
build. Those concern \emph{causes} --- the laser is under-delivering, the
recoater short-feeding, the gas flow degraded --- and which it is determines what
gets corrected. The distinction is not semantic, because causes can be mutually
indistinguishable in a way outcomes are not: if two of them drive the readings
along the same direction in signal space, the readings carry no information
separating them and no classifier, however trained, recovers the difference. That
limit is structural, a property of the measurement and the physics rather than of
the algorithm, where a pair separable in principle but misclassified in practice
is an algorithm that can be improved.

Diagnosability analysis is the established discipline for deciding which case
holds. Developed within model-based fault detection and isolation, it asks,
before any estimator is built, which faults a measurement set can detect and
which it can isolate from one another \cite{Krysander2008}. The methods
are standard where sensor sets are costly and must be justified in advance, but
to the authors' knowledge have not been applied to LPBF.

This paper applies them. A calibrated reduced-order thermal model is driven by
five injected fault modes and the signatures compared pairwise against noise,
yielding a diagnosability matrix per configuration that divides fault pairs into
those resolvable from existing signals, those needing an added measurement and
those structurally irreducible. The contributions are the first such matrix for
the process, to the authors' knowledge; noise figures derived from each sensor's
transduction chain rather than assumed; a demonstration that laser power and gas
degradation are \emph{exactly} confounded instantaneously; the finding that the
binding limit is the melt pool rather than the instrumentation; and that build
structure already present dissolves it at no hardware cost.

\section{Background and related work}

\subsection{What in-situ monitoring classifies}

Melt pool monitoring with coaxial photodiodes and cameras is the most widely
deployed configuration, and its canonical application is flaw classification.
Scime and Beuth identified signatures indicative of keyholing porosity and
balling from high-speed visible imaging \cite{Scime2019}, and later work extended
this across modalities and alloys, including mechanical-property prediction from
coaxial signals \cite{Raj2023}. The predicted quantity is in each case an outcome
or a material state, and whether two distinct excursions could have produced the
same signal is never posed.

A smaller body of work estimates process inputs and is closer to the present
question. RAISE-LPBF reconstructs laser power and scan speed independently from
on-axis video on 316L, sampling both per scan line from continuous distributions
so interactions can be studied \cite{Blanc2024}. Reijonen reports coaxial
photodiode response to power, speed, focal position, scan orientation and
shielding gas flow, finding power approximately linear, speed a power law and the
remainder non-linear and interactive \cite{Reijonen2024}. Both characterise
\emph{sensitivity}; identifiability --- whether sensitivities to different causes
are linearly distinguishable --- is what neither addresses.

A third class observes the powder bed, attacking causes the melt pool reports
only indirectly \cite{Liu2022,Bugatti2022}, and returns in
Section~\ref{sec:added} as a target rather than a modelled option.

\subsection{Lumped process metrics are incomplete}

A recurring pattern is to report a \emph{lumped} process quantity in place of its
components. Volumetric energy density (VED) is the usual choice, and
state-of-the-art parameter estimation from melt pool imagery still predicts
energy density \emph{class} rather than power and speed separately \cite{kNN2025}.

There is direct evidence that this discards information: Guo et al.\ held input
energy density constant while varying power and speed together and saw melt pool
dimensions change by orders of magnitude with transitions between melting
regimes \cite{Guo2019}, and the 2022 Additive Manufacturing Benchmark found
single-track depth correlated with VED while width did not \cite{Weaver2024}.
Both are reproduced in Section~\ref{sec:valid}. Falling back on a lumped group is
the natural response to confounded components, and whether they are confounded
for a given suite is the diagnosability question the literature has not asked.

\subsection{Diagnosability analysis}

Model-based diagnosability analysis operates on a model expressed over known
variables, unknown variables and faults. Structural analysis considers only which
variables appear in which equations, determining detectability and isolability
from the resulting bipartite graph, with minimal structurally overdetermined
(MSO) sets identifying the redundant subsystems from which consistency tests are
built \cite{Krysander2008}. Being purely combinatorial it is insensitive to
parameter error but can be optimistic: two faults may be structurally isolable
yet numerically indistinguishable. Quantitative analysis supplies the remainder
by linearising the fault-to-measurement map and examining the geometry of the
signature directions; this paper runs both.

\section{Method}
%% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Forward model and calibration}

Melt pool geometry is computed from the Eagar--Tsai solution for a moving Gaussian
heat source on a semi-infinite solid, extended with the Gladush--Smurov keyhole
depth correction following Honarmandi et al.\ \cite{Hon2021}. Four parameters ---
effective absorptivity, effective conductivity, heat capacity, the keyhole
constant --- are fitted to the seven IN718 bare-plate single-track cases of the
2022 Additive Manufacturing Benchmark \cite{Weaver2024}, giving \textbf{11.7\,\% mean error on melt pool width and 13.1\,\% on depth}. All results below are evaluated at that benchmark's own baseline condition,
285\,W, 960\,mm\,s$^{-1}$ and a 67\,\textmu m D4$\sigma$ spot, where the errors
are $-15.5$\,\% on width and $+1.7$\,\% on depth against expanded uncertainties
($k=2$) of 6.2 and 14.1\,\textmu m respectively. Width is under-predicted at all seven conditions, the one-sided bias of a
conduction solution with no mechanism that widens a pool. The keyhole correction
is necessary rather than optional: all seven cases keyhole, depth-to-width
ratios running 0.72--2.14 against the criterion's 0.67, and a pure conduction
fit given the same three free parameters leaves 23.6\,\% depth error against
13.1\,\%, at an essentially unchanged 8.5\,\% on width. The quadrature in the
history integral and the derivative step are both set by convergence tests:
central differences are evaluated at $h=0.002$, entries still moving 39.3\,\%
between $h=0.04$ and $h=0.02$ but below 0.3\,\% at $h=0.005$, the residual
following the predicted second-order scaling.

\subsection{Fault definitions}

Five causes are modelled, each defined as a \emph{physical} quantity acting on a
model input so no arbitrary severity-to-signal constant is introduced. For
magnitude $f$: \emph{power} scales laser power, $P(1+f)$; \emph{speed} scales
scan speed, $v(1+f)$; \emph{gas} is a plume optical depth attenuating effective
absorptivity, $A\,e^{-f}$; \emph{geometry} is a preheat rise in units of 100\,K,
$T_0+100f$; \emph{layer} is a fractional layer-thickness deviation, positive
denoting a \emph{thinner} layer as produced by recoater short-feed, acting
through three channels.

Two of those three channels derive from published measurement. The absorptivity
coupling, $-0.230$, follows from the powder-to-solid absorptivity ratio of
approximately two measured calorimetrically at 1070\,nm \cite{Trapp2017} with a
radiative penetration depth of a few particle diameters, implying a bare-solid
absorptivity of 0.258, inside the 0.25--0.35 reported for nickel alloys near
1\,\textmu m --- a consistency the calibration was not built to produce. The powder-melting
enthalpy coupling, $+0.186$, follows from an energy balance in which melting the
layer consumes 15.7\,\% of the absorbed energy, so a thinner layer frees
proportionally more for penetration. The conductivity coupling remains an unmeasured estimate, swept across its range
in Section~\ref{sec:robust} with every accumulated-regime result invariant to it,
but it is load-bearing instantaneously: the other two channels nearly cancel on
the absorbed-power product ($-0.230+0.186=-0.044$), so without it the layer fault
acts only through that product and joins the power/gas degeneracy
\emph{exactly} --- its Jacobian column becomes $-0.044$ times the power column on
all three sensors, and power/layer and layer/gas both read $0.00^\circ$. This one
unmeasured coupling is all that distinguishes a thin layer from a weak laser at
an instant. All couplings are applied in exponential form $A\,e^{cf}$, agreeing with
$A(1+cf)$ to first order but unable to pass through zero, where the linear form
would drive thermal diffusivity negative and the integrand imaginary.

\subsection{Sensor models}

Three coaxial observables are computed from the temperature field: a
band-integrated photodiode (700--1000\,nm), melt pool area, and a two-colour
pyrometer (750/950\,nm) evaluated as the ratio of integrated band powers and
inverted for temperature. Spectral radiance follows Planck's law, temperature
being capped at the boiling point before any radiance is computed. Four
candidates are defined for Section~\ref{sec:melt}: the camera's width and length
reported separately, a short-wave infrared photodiode and a wide-band
pyrometer.

\subsection{Diagnosability measures}

The relative-sensitivity Jacobian is
%
\begin{equation}
J_{sk} \;=\; \frac{1}{y_s}\,\frac{\partial y_s}{\partial f_k},
\label{eq:jac}
\end{equation}
%
so entry $(s,k)$ is the fractional change in sensor $s$ per unit of fault $k$.
Each row is then
divided by that sensor's relative noise $\sigma_s$, derived in
Section~\ref{sec:noise}, to give the whitened matrix $J^{\mathrm{w}}$: only then
are rows in a common unit and angles between columns physically meaningful.

Three quantities are reported throughout. The \emph{angle} between whitened
columns, $\theta_{kl} = \arccos\big(|\langle J^{\mathrm{w}}_{:k},
J^{\mathrm{w}}_{:l}\rangle| / \|J^{\mathrm{w}}_{:k}\|
\|J^{\mathrm{w}}_{:l}\|\big)$, measures pairwise separability: $0^\circ$ means
the two faults move the readings along one line and are indistinguishable by any
estimator, $90^\circ$ that they are orthogonal. A practical floor of $10^\circ$
is adopted as a convention and its influence reported. The \emph{rank} of
$J^{\mathrm{w}}$ counts how many fault directions are observable at all, treating
a singular value below $10^{-6}$ of the largest as none, and the \emph{condition
number} how thin the weakest retained one is; rank without it is misleading, as
Section~\ref{sec:melt} shows. Structural analysis is run in parallel on an
eleven-equation representation using MSO sets, as an independent check.

\subsection{Build context and the enlarged observation}
\label{sec:context}

A build is not a single instant. It comprises thousands of segments whose
toolpath, layer index, gas-flow direction and local geometry are known to the
machine, and the five faults do not vary identically across them: laser
degradation drifts with time, galvanometer error concentrates at direction
reversals, a recoater short-feed recurs with layer index, gas degradation follows
the flow direction, heat accumulation follows support depth. Treat the build
rather than the instant as the observation: let $G\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 5}$ hold
five known context regressors over $N$ segments --- a constant, position along
the gas-flow axis, layer parity, turn density, support depth --- and
$M\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 5}$ the modulation profile of each fault. Regressing
each sensor's relative residual onto $G$ gives five coefficients per sensor in
place of one reading, so three sensors give 15 observables, with Jacobian
%
\begin{equation}
J^{\mathrm{big}}_{(s,j),k} = J_{sk}\,B_{jk},
\qquad B = (G^{\!\top}G)^{-1}G^{\!\top}M .
\label{eq:big}
\end{equation}
%
The noise on the $j$-th coefficient from sensor $s$ is not $\sigma_s$ but
$\sigma_{(s,j)} = \sigma_s\sqrt{[(G^{\!\top}G)^{-1}]_{jj}}$, the square-root
factor running 0.109 to 0.136, so fitting $N=84$ segments to five regressors
buys a seven- to ninefold noise reduction. Results use $N=84$, with the dependence on $N$ in
Section~\ref{sec:robust}.

The profiles in $M$ are physical claims rather than measurements, and they are
\emph{machine-specific}: a different nozzle geometry, scan strategy or part gives
different patterns, so there is no universal set of five to go and find. One of
them can nonetheless be calibrated from published data, and the inversion is the
recipe by which a reader calibrates the rest on their own machine. The AM Bench pad scans \cite{Weaver2024}, under a 4.3\,m\,s$^{-1}$ cross-flow,
show melt pool depth falling from 175 to 125\,\textmu m on tracks scanned
parallel to the gas flow against anti-parallel ones --- a 27.2\,\% mean
reduction against standard deviations of 4--11\,\textmu m --- with perpendicular pads as a control at 2.4--4.7\,\%. Weaver et al.\ attribute
the reduction to plume by-products attenuating, scattering and lensing the beam,
and report that none of the four challenge submissions predicted it; this model
represents the attenuation alone. Inverting each depth change
through the model's gas channel at the benchmark's baseline condition --- where
the model's depth error is $+1.7$\,\% against $-15.5$\,\% on width --- gives
$f_{\mathrm{gas}}=0.046$ for the control against $0.404$ for the parallel mean,
an \textbf{8.8$\times$ swing within one healthy build} against the 6.05$\times$
assumed here. In contrast units that is 1.08 where this work assumes 0.90, so
\textbf{the one profile that can be checked is understated by 20\,\%}. Three qualifications: the driver is scan direction rather than position along the
flow, it is baseline plume behaviour rather than a fault, and the inversion is
model-mediated. The gas signature also alternates at track scale where a recoater
signature alternates at layer scale --- the distinctness
Section~\ref{sec:robust} shows the method needs.

\subsection{Sensor noise, derived from transduction}
\label{sec:noise}

Whitening is half of the measurement geometry, so an asserted noise set silently
decides the answer. Only one in-process figure proved citable, a 2.3\,\% relative
width uncertainty from a cross-sectional metrology budget \cite{Weaver2024},
which is not in-process noise. The figures are therefore \emph{derived} from one
assumption --- each radiometric channel carries the same fractional noise
$\varepsilon$ --- with the rest following from how each instrument turns radiance
into its reading. Only ratios enter the whitening, so $\varepsilon$ cancels and
no absolute threshold is claimed.

The photodiode reading \emph{is} a band-integrated radiometric power, so
$\sigma_{\mathrm{pd}} = \varepsilon$ and it serves as the unit. The pyrometer
inverts a ratio of two band powers: independent channel noise puts
$\sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon$ on the ratio, and the inversion divides by the ratio's
Wien temperature sensitivity $|\partial \ln R/\partial \ln T| = (c_2/T)
(\lambda_2^{-1}-\lambda_1^{-1}) = 1.333$ at 750/950\,nm and the model's 3031\,K,
giving $\sigma_{\mathrm{pyr}} = 1.061\,\varepsilon$. Closely spaced bands discard most of the
available temperature sensitivity and the inversion amplifies what noise remains,
so a two-colour pyrometer is a \emph{noisier} relative instrument than the
photodiode it is built from. The camera reports an extent found
by thresholding at the liquidus, where a fractional radiance error is
indistinguishable from the threshold sitting at a different temperature,
$\delta \ln T_{\mathrm{liq}} = -\varepsilon/(\partial \ln L/\partial \ln T)$.
Planck is steep there, $\partial \ln L/\partial \ln T = 10.52$ at 850\,nm and
1609\,K, so $\varepsilon = 1\,\%$ displaces the apparent isotherm by only
0.095\,\% in temperature, and propagating that shift through the model's own
dimension solver gives $\sigma_{\mathrm{cam}} = 0.142\,\varepsilon$: thresholding
on a steep Planck function localises an edge well, making the camera the most
precise of the three by a factor of seven.

The derived set is $1 : 0.142 : 1.061$, against the $1 : 1 : 0.03$ assumed before
deriving it --- wrong in both free entries and in opposite directions. Two choices
inside the derivation were taken in the direction that costs this analysis: the
area channel is not averaged along the pool perimeter, and width and length are
whitened as independent although a common-mode error correlates them, which
understates the value of reporting them separately. Both limits are swept,
alongside a gain-dominated case in which the pyrometer's ratio rejects
common-mode drift and approaches the 0.03 once assumed.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Validation}
\label{sec:valid}

One external check and one of internal structure. Against the
constant-energy-density experiment of Guo et al.\ \cite{Guo2019} the model
achieves a \textbf{partial pass}, reproducing the existence, direction and regime
transition of the variation but spanning 2.37$\times$ in depth where the
experiment reports orders of magnitude --- the mechanism they identify,
absorption varying with vapour depression geometry, being absent from a
conduction solution. The same sweep yields a result used in
Section~\ref{sec:added}: at constant energy density melt pool \emph{length}
varies by 2.53$\times$ against 1.11$\times$ for width and depth.

The second asks whether the model reproduces the benchmark's reported structure,
depth tracking volumetric energy density while width does not \cite{Weaver2024}.
As a correlation it is unreliable --- over 27 melted conditions the model gives
$r=+0.20$ for width against $+0.77$ for depth, but width's coefficient runs
$+0.68$ to $-0.22$ across five such grids, a property of the sampled region
rather than of the model. The exponents are stable where the correlation is not.
Width and depth are each a power law in $(P,v,d)$ to $R^{2}\ge0.94$, and a pure function
of $\mathrm{VED}=P/(vd^{2})$ must have exponents along $(1,-1,-2)$: on every grid
depth's lie at $\cos 0.64$--$0.74$ of that ray against width's $-0.11$ to
$0.00$, because the two depend on spot size with opposite signs --- the one
variable a volumetric density folds away. All 27 points are model output, so this
is a consistency check, not external validation.

\subsection{Laser power and shielding gas are exactly confounded}
\label{sec:confound}

Both faults act \emph{only} on the product (effective absorptivity $\times$
power), in which the Eagar--Tsai temperature rise is linear. Gas attenuates
absorptivity as $A\,e^{-f_{\mathrm{gas}}}$ and power scales as
$P(1+f_{\mathrm{power}})$, so the product is unchanged whenever
$f_{\mathrm{gas}}=\ln(1+f_{\mathrm{power}})$, giving an identical melt pool ---
the same temperature at every point, hence identical readings from any instrument
that could be built --- the two faults cancel bit-identically in double
precision on all three. The
ratio of the two Jacobian columns is $-0.99999$, $-1.00000$, $-0.99993$ across
the sensors, a spread of $7\times10^{-5}$ from finite-difference error on an
exact identity, and the pairwise angle is \textbf{0.00$^\circ$}. The degeneracy
is algebraic, not numerical: sweeping every coupling constant across its
plausible range leaves it at $0.00^\circ$, and the structural analysis identifies
the same pair from equation form alone.

\subsection{The limit is the melt pool, not the sensor count}
\label{sec:melt}

The three observables are not independent. Normalising the Jacobian's sensor Gram
matrix $JJ^{\mathsf T}$ gives pairwise correlations of \textbf{0.99960}
(photodiode--camera), \textbf{0.99508} (photodiode--pyrometer) and
\textbf{0.99477} (camera--pyrometer): a photodiode reading band radiance, a
camera reading pool area in square metres and a two-colour pyrometer reading
kelvin respond to the five faults in patterns agreeing to better than 0.994. Row
scalings cancel in the normalisation, so this is the one result here that does
\emph{not} depend on the derived noise figures.

The singular values quantify those correlations rather than establishing them
independently --- 13.14, 0.0638 and 0.00513, so 99.998\,\% of the suite's squared
response lies along a single direction. Three of the five fault directions are
observed and \textbf{two are not observed at all}, the unobserved pair spanning
the power--gas combination of Section~\ref{sec:confound}; of the three observed,
the weakest responds \textbf{2562} times less than the strongest. Adding all four
candidate observables gives seven rows and does not raise the attained rank:
the singular values become $(23.7,\ 9.74,\ 6.0\times10^{-2},\ 5.0\times10^{-6},\
1.4\times10^{-6})$, the fourth at $2\times10^{-7}$ of the leading one, two orders
below the $10^{-6}$ threshold adopted here, so four directions are formally
present but only three are measurements. \textbf{Seven instruments leave power
and gas exactly as confounded as one.}

The reason is structural. Everything the model knows funnels into four physical
channels --- absorbed power, scan speed, effective conductivity and preheat ---
with three of the five faults crowding onto the first. Any instrument reading
only the melt pool is a function of those four channels, so the fault-to-sensor
map cannot exceed rank 4 however many are fitted, and at realistic noise it does
not reach even that: a new wavelength reweights the same Planck curve over the
same pool, adding a row, not a direction.

\subsection{Build context dissolves the limit}

With the enlarged observation of Section~\ref{sec:context}, rank rises from 3 to
\textbf{5}, the worst pair from $0.00^\circ$ to \textbf{24.90$^\circ$}, power
versus gas to $29.34^\circ$, and the condition number falls from 2562 to 50 ---
all ten pairs clearing the $10^\circ$ floor (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}) against none
per segment.

Three controls establish that this arises from the claimed mechanism rather than
the arithmetic. Most important, giving all five faults one identical profile
returns the instantaneous result \emph{exactly} --- rank 3, condition number 2562,
worst pair $0.00^\circ$ --- despite the enlarged matrix still having 15 rows,
because proportional columns of $B$ make it inherit the instantaneous column
directions: \textbf{the gain is not dimensionality, it is the profiles
differing}. Replacing every derivative by a secant through the full nonlinear
chain gives rank 5 at every magnitude from 0.2\,\% to 20\,\% in both signs, worst
pair $12.82^\circ$--$37.95^\circ$ and condition number 47--62, so the derivative
limit is \emph{not} strictly conservative: a $+20\,\%$ fault gives $12.82^\circ$,
above the floor by only 1.3$\times$. Recomputing with the full model at every
segment reproduces the angles to $0.04^\circ$.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:robust}

Because the profiles are machine-specific, the primary result is separability
\emph{as a function of} profile strength: five of ten pairs clear the floor at or
below 25\,\% of the strength assumed here and the hardest, power/speed, at
\textbf{45\,\%}, so a machine whose patterns are half as strong still resolves
every pair --- and the one profile calibrated against measurement came back
20\,\% \emph{stronger} than assumed. The headline moves by under half a degree
across the full range of the unmeasured conductivity coupling and all four
noise sets of Section~\ref{sec:noise}. Reassigning which fault follows which
build variable moves it further, the worst pair running $16.2^\circ$ to
$30.1^\circ$ across all 120 permutations, but \textbf{every one of the 120
leaves rank five and all ten pairs above the floor} --- the result needs the
patterns to differ, not any particular fault to own any particular one.
Segment count is the one real requirement, at $11.23^\circ$ for $N=12$ and
$22.90^\circ$ for $N=30$ on a near-square plan, flat thereafter.

What the method requires is not accurate contrasts but \emph{distinct} patterns,
and at these noise figures strictly so: forcing any two equal collapses that pair
to its instantaneous angle, and every instantaneous angle is below the floor, the
mildest leaving $4.63^\circ$. \textbf{No pair of faults may share a spatial
signature.} None does on this machine --- the recoater advances once per layer
along a fixed axis while the gas flows along a fixed chamber axis, and the
benchmark measurement of Section~\ref{sec:context} confirms they alternate at
different scales --- and establishing that on another machine is a far weaker
requirement than measuring five contrasts.

Distinctness also tolerates a profile being attributed to the wrong build
variable, which matters because measured layer-thickness variation is reported
to follow part support rather than the recoater cycle assumed here
\cite{Liu2022}. Mixing the geometry fault's support driver into the layer
profile leaves layer/geometry above the floor until that profile is roughly
84\,\% support-driven ($12.07^\circ$ at 80\,\%), and mixing the power fault's
build-drift driver never brings power/layer below it at any fraction, since
every profile is normalised to unit mean and two faults sharing a driver still
differ wherever their contrasts do.

\subsection{Diagnosability matrix and configuration classes}

Classifying on the enlarged observation alone places all ten pairs in class~1 for
every configuration, a single photodiode included --- true but uninformative. Two
regimes are therefore reported (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}): \emph{instantaneous},
per-segment decisions for closed-loop control, and \emph{accumulated}, diagnosis
over a build for qualification and drift monitoring. The baseline suite resolves none of the ten instantaneously and all ten
accumulated, class~3 empty in both.

Five commercial systems were surveyed and present only three distinct
observable sets. Those are reported as classes rather than products, alongside
a single-photodiode floor and the research reference: \textbf{A}, a single
coaxial photodiode (rank 1); \textbf{B}, photodiode plus pool imaging, and
\textbf{C}, two photodiodes in separate bands (rank 2); \textbf{D}, the research
reference adding pyrometry, and \textbf{E}, C plus upstream power monitoring
(rank 3). No vendor is named: mapping a product to a set of observables rests on
secondary literature rather than datasheets, detection bands being withheld and
published descriptions of one product disagreeing.

\textbf{No configuration built solely from melt-pool observables separates laser
power from gas degradation}, whatever its sensor count: A through D all read
$0.00^\circ$ on that pair, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:melt}, and at
the derived noise figures none resolves \emph{any} pair per segment. E is the
actionable class --- a measurement of delivered laser power sampled
\emph{upstream} of the process zone separates the pair at $84.02^\circ$ and
resolves four of ten, seeing the laser output without the plume attenuation. One
surveyed system may belong at E rather than C depending on whether its
laser-energy photodiode samples upstream, and is classed by what public
documentation states rather than by what is likely. B and C are separated not by
class count, both empty, but by correlation: two bands of the same emitted light
agree at 0.99996 against 0.99960 for a geometric measurement.

\subsection{What an added measurement would buy}
\label{sec:added}

The sensors most likely to help --- plume sensing for gas, powder-bed or
recoater sensing for layer \cite{Liu2022,Bugatti2022} --- cannot be simulated by
a model with neither a plume nor a powder layer, but what such a sensor
\emph{measures} can be: a unit row on fault $k$ at a given relative noise, the
idealised best case for any instrument aimed at that cause.

A direct measurement of any one cause raises the rank to 4 and clears exactly the
four pairs involving that cause --- no more. Geometry is the exception, raising
the rank not at all: a unit row on geometry already lies \emph{entirely} within
the row space of the existing three (projection 1.0000, against 0.53--0.91 for
the others), so the suite observes preheat already and simply cannot separate the
rest. \textbf{Precision is irrelevant}: sweeping the added sensor's noise from
50\,\% to 0.2\,\% leaves the worst pair at $0.32^\circ$ and four of ten cleared
throughout, since adding a row in a new direction changes the rank while making
an existing row quieter does not. \textbf{Build context outperforms two
idealised, perfect direct measurements of the causes themselves}: gas and layer
sensors together give rank 5 but $0.70^\circ$ and seven of ten, against
$24.90^\circ$ and ten of ten for build context at no cost --- neither says
anything about power versus speed.

Among melt-pool observables the most valuable addition is free, and is the
strongest practical result here. Reporting the camera's two axes separately
rather than as an area is worth \textbf{+23.89$^\circ$} on the pair that binds
the baseline accumulated result ($24.90^\circ$ to $48.79^\circ$), after which
power/geometry binds instead at $25.26^\circ$, so the suite's worst accumulated
pair gains only $0.35^\circ$. What the split buys outright is \emph{per
segment}: it is the only change resolving anything there, taking that count
from none of ten to \textbf{seven of ten} (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}); a short-wave infrared photodiode
($+0.01^\circ$) and a wide-band pyrometer ($0.00^\circ$) are worth nothing. The
mechanism is independence, not responsiveness: width is both the candidate least
correlated with the coaxial photodiode, 0.779 against 0.979 for length, and the
better localised, its edge on a steep lateral gradient where the trailing edge
lies in the pool's thermal tail (Section~\ref{sec:noise}). An area averages the
informative axis against the uninformative one and buries it, while
responsiveness runs the other way --- length carries 2.5$\times$ the variation of
width (Section~\ref{sec:valid}) and still adds less.

Failed and successful candidates differ by one criterion: \emph{a sensor added to
break a confound must observe upstream of the shared channel; a sensor reading
the melt pool adds a row, not a direction.}

\section{Discussion}
%% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Relation to reported monitoring performance}

Comparing predicted limits against reported accuracies proves largely
unrealisable, and the reason is itself a result. Of 19 works surveyed --- in-situ
LPBF monitoring with a stated input signal and output, 2019--2025 --- seven
classify outcomes and only \textbf{three estimate a process input from an in-situ
signal}, one of those a lumped energy-density class. None analyses whether two
causes are separable, and the one work varying power, speed and gas flow on a
single photodiode \cite{Reijonen2024} does not frame the question that way. That is not an accident of what the field chose
to study: outcomes are functions of the melt pool, and the melt pool is a
function of the four channels of Section~\ref{sec:melt}, so outcome
classification cannot separate two causes sharing a channel, for the same reason
no melt-pool instrument can. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, but
no published result contradicts the predicted limits, and two groups
independently report the lumped group this analysis identifies as what survives
confounding \cite{Blanc2024,kNN2025}.

The one external check on this model's sensor response can be made, and it
partly fails. Reijonen reports the coaxial photodiode response to scan speed as
$y=ax^{-b}$ with $b=0.337$ at 400\,W over 400--1200\,mm\,s$^{-1}$
\cite{Reijonen2024}. The comparable quantity is a power law fitted to this model
over \emph{their} range rather than the Jacobian entry $-0.483$; that fit gives
\textbf{0.468}, some 39\,\% too steep. The qualitative comparison is worse: they
find $b$ growing with laser power, ``almost non-existent'' at 100\,W and
reaching 0.337 at 400\,W, where this model gives 0.51, 0.44 and 0.47 at 100, 285
and 400\,W --- flat.

\textbf{The discrepancy lies in physics this model assumes away.} Reijonen
attributes the speed dependence to the vapour plume: the trend holds only where
gas flow is high and constant, and at low power, in conduction mode, there is no
plume and no speed effect at all. Here the plume is a \emph{fixed} optical depth,
independent of power and speed and emitting nothing of its own, so no mechanism
exists by which $b$ could grow with power. That same assumption is what makes
the power/gas degeneracy exact (Section~\ref{sec:confound}), and these
measurements show the real attenuation depends on both parameters and can even
reverse sign with scan speed at low gas flow. \textbf{The reported $0.00^\circ$
is an upper bound on the instantaneous confounding on external evidence, not
merely by assertion.}

Three caveats on the comparison: it crosses material (316L against IN718),
detection band (a shortpass below 800\,nm against 700--1000\,nm) and bed
condition (30\,\textmu m powder layers against bare plate). One point in the
model's defence: it is calibrated at 285\,W where all seven benchmark cases
keyhole, so the fixed optical depth is a local approximation \emph{inside} the
regime where a plume exists rather than a claim about conduction-mode melting.
And on the power response, Reijonen reports a linear fit $y=ax+b$ without
quoting the intercept; since relative sensitivity is then $aP/(aP+b)$, equal to
one only through the origin, this model's $+1.52$ cannot be called a conflict
until that intercept is known.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The thermal model is a conduction solution with an analytic keyhole patch: it
partial-passes the Guo validation, its fitted parameters sit at their bounds, and
the absorptivity, conductivity and heat capacity correlations of 0.93--0.96
reported during Bayesian calibration of the same model family \cite{Hon2021} are
the pathology this paper studies appearing in its own calibration. Three
modelling choices then make the instantaneous case \emph{more} confounded than
reality: the gas fault as pure optical depth omits a convective channel; treating
plume attenuation as independent of power and speed is what makes the degeneracy
exact; and modelling upstream power monitoring as a melt-pool-derived signal is
likewise pessimistic.

\textbf{The $0.00^\circ$ is therefore an upper bound, and its exactness is an
artefact of the one-channel gas model.} Gas here acts only on absorbed power,
precisely the channel a power fault acts on, which is what makes the two
algebraically identical. A real plume also scatters: Reijonen et al.\ report
reduced gas flow giving loss of penetration together with \emph{increased} melt
pool width \cite{Reijonen2020}, where attenuation alone gives a decrease ---
$-10$\,\% here at $f_{\mathrm{gas}}=0.4$. Adding that broadening as a spot-size
channel, which power does not possess, opens the pair to \textbf{2--3$^\circ$}
at the strength needed to reverse the width sign, and leaves the suite's worst
pair unmoved at $0.32^\circ$. The exactness is a property of the model; the
inseparability is not. The rank limit is unaffected either way, deriving from
channel counting rather than the form of this coupling.

The geometry inversion fails, returning $+687$\,K of preheat because a pad
differs from a single track by remelting as well as preheat, and no dataset yet
offers a build with \emph{deliberately induced} machine faults: every inversion
here reads a healthy build and extrapolates, which is the natural follow-on
experiment. The noise derivation rests on one assumption, that every radiometric
channel carries the same fractional noise, bounded by the gain-dominated sweep.
The five fault magnitudes are physical but not on a common severity scale, so
comparisons \emph{across pairs at fixed magnitude} are not meaningful, and all
results are diagnosability statements --- what a suite can distinguish in
principle, at a given relative noise, under a given forward model --- not that
any algorithm achieves it.

%% ===========================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
%% ===========================================================================

Laser power deviation and shielding gas degradation produce identical melt pools
in this model, confounded at $0.00^\circ$ by algebra rather than noise, so no
instrument reading the melt pool separates them from a single instant. The
binding limit is the melt pool's four physical channels, not the instrumentation:
at noise derived from each sensor's transduction chain, seven observables attain
rank 3 against that bound of 4 and leave the worst pair at $0.00^\circ$. Deriving
those figures rather than assuming them matters, whitening being half of the
measurement geometry --- the set assumed before deriving it was wrong in both
free entries and in opposite directions.

Build structure already present dissolves the limit at no hardware cost --- rank
3 $\rightarrow$ 5, worst pair $0.00^\circ \rightarrow 24.90^\circ$, all ten pairs
resolved where none is per segment --- surviving at half the assumed profile
strength, under all four noise sets, at fault magnitudes from 0.2\,\% to
20\,\%, and in all 120 reassignments of profile to fault, with a shared-profile
control reproducing the instantaneous result exactly. It outperforms two idealised direct measurements of
the causes themselves and requires only that no two faults share a spatial
signature; those patterns being machine-specific, the deliverable is the method
plus the strength a machine needs, with the inversion of
Section~\ref{sec:context} as the recipe for calibrating it.

For real-time control an upstream measurement of delivered laser power is the
recommendation, separating power from gas at $84^\circ$ where no melt-pool
configuration separates them at all; among melt-pool observables the most
valuable addition is free, reporting the camera's two axes separately being
worth $+23.89^\circ$ on the pair that binds the baseline and being the only
change resolving anything per segment. Post-build
diagnosis needs no new hardware, real-time isolation does, and crude sensing of
the right cause suffices. The method needs only a forward model and a sensor
description, and answers at specification time what is otherwise settled after
the hardware is bought.
