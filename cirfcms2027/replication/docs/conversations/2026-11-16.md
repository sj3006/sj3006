## The one assumption

Every radiometric channel carries the same fractional noise ε. Nothing else is assumed, and only *ratios* enter the whitening, so ε cancels and no absolute noise level is ever claimed. The three numbers come from how each instrument converts radiance into a reading.

## Photodiode — the unit

It sums the light in its band. A 1% error in the light is a 1% error in the reading, with nothing in between.

$$\sigma_{\text{pd}} = \varepsilon$$

Everything else is quoted against this.

## Camera — 0.142

It reports melt pool **area**, found by thresholding the image at the liquidus. The key step: a brightness error is indistinguishable from the liquidus isotherm sitting at a slightly different temperature. So convert brightness noise into an apparent temperature error by dividing by how steeply Planck's law climbs:

$$\delta \ln T = \frac{-\varepsilon}{\partial \ln L / \partial \ln T}$$

At 850 nm and the liquidus, $\partial \ln L/\partial \ln T = 10.52$: a 1% temperature change moves the radiance 10.52%. Planck is steep there, so the division **shrinks** the error — ε = 1% displaces the apparent isotherm by only 0.095% in temperature.

Push that through the model's own dimension solver and the isotherm moves width by 0.030% and length by 0.112%. Area is their product, and one threshold shift moves both together, so the fractional errors add rather than combining in quadrature:

$$\sigma_{\text{cam}} = 0.030 + 0.112 = 0.142\,\varepsilon$$

Thresholding on a steep function localises an edge well. That is why the camera is the quietest of the three, by a factor of seven.

## Two-colour pyrometer — 1.061

It divides the power in one band by the power in another and inverts the ratio for temperature. Two steps, pulling opposite ways:

1. **Two independent channel errors**, so the ratio carries $\sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon$.
2. **Invert the ratio.** Its sensitivity is $\left|\frac{d\ln R}{d\ln T}\right| = \frac{c_2}{T}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_2} - \frac{1}{\lambda_1}\right) = 1.333$ at 750/950 nm and 3031 K.

$$\sigma_{\text{pyr}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{1.333}\,\varepsilon = 1.061\,\varepsilon$$

So the inferred temperature is *slightly noisier than the photodiode it is built from*. Dividing by 1.333 does reduce the √2, just not all the way back. The cause is that 750 and 950 nm sit close together: either band **alone** changes 5–6% per 1% of temperature, so the ratio keeps under a quarter of the sensitivity available. A single calibrated band would do five to six times better. That is the price of the ratio's one virtue, needing no absolute calibration.

## The set

$$1 : 0.142 : 1.061$$

And this is not a detail applied after the geometry — it *is* half the geometry. Whitening divides each sensor's Jacobian row by its own σ, which is what puts a photodiode's radiometric power, a camera's square metres and a pyrometer's kelvin into one common unit so the angles between fault signatures mean anything at all.

### Claude, 07:51

Your paper has 19 `\emph`, no `\textit`, `\textsl` or `\itshape`, and nothing italic in the bibliography. So the body is one line:

```latex
\makeatletter
\let\realemph\emph                         % handle for places that must stay
\DeclareRobustCommand{\emph}[1]{#1}
\DeclareRobustCommand{\textit}[1]{#1}      % unused here, but future-proofs a paste
\DeclareRobustCommand{\textsl}[1]{#1}
\makeatother
```

Put it before `\begin{document)`. `\DeclareRobustCommand` rather than `\renewcommand` because `\emph` is declared robust and a plain `\renewcommand` will warn. Math is untouched: variable italics come from the math font, not from these commands, so `$\psi_k$` and `$J_w$` stay as they are.

## Two things it will not catch

**Your 29 subsection headings are italic**, and not through `\emph`. `elsarticle` sets them that way in its own sectioning style, so flattening `\emph` leaves them exactly as they were. If you want those upright too, change `\itshape` to `\normalfont` in the `\subsection` and `\subsubsection` lines of the `compact-spacing.tex` block I gave you earlier:

```latex
\renewcommand\subsection{\@startsection{subsection}{2}{\z@}%
           {7\p@ \@plus 2\p@ \@minus 2\p@}{1\p@}%
           {\normalfont\normalsize}}          % was \normalfont\normalsize\itshape
```

Be aware that with the italics gone a subsection heading becomes indistinguishable from body text, since `elsarticle` gives it no other marker — no bold, no size change, no numbering style of its own. I would leave the headings alone.
